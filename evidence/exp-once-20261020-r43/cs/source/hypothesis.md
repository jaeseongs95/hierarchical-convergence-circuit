# Core Hypothesis

## HCC core statement

Cognition may repeatedly use a common computational primitive across different abstraction levels:

```text
activation
→ binding
→ competition / inhibition
→ recurrent correction
→ convergence
```

A converged state at one abstraction level may become a candidate node at a higher level. Processing is not assumed to be a simple bottom-up serial pipeline; top-down and recurrent interaction are part of the working model.

For novel or unresolved inputs, the system may expand a dynamic graph and adapt A/D/M. Repeatedly successful graphs may be validated, reinforced, compressed, and reused as circuits. Familiar processing may therefore converge rapidly through compiled circuits, while novelty, contradiction, low convergence margin, or external mismatch may reopen graph search.

## Strong extensions

The following are working hypotheses, not established facts:

- perception and high-level reasoning can reuse the same class of convergence primitive,
- intuition can be modeled as fast high-A convergence through compiled circuits,
- explicit reasoning can be modeled as graph expansion when compiled circuits are insufficient,
- illusion and bad intuition can share the higher-level error form `stable misconvergence`,
- learning can be modeled as a bidirectional `Graph ↔ Circuit` process,
- stronger attractors may trade speed for revisability.
